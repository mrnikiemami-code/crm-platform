# Designer brief 01 — independent diagnosis - Superdesign

You are the product designer for an internal Persian Farsi RTL B2B CRM built on Twenty. The engineering team is NOT allowed to prescribe the visual solution; the diagnosis and the direction are yours.

## What happened

The screen under review is the "مالیات و عوارض" Tax and Duty Policies settings panel. It is a single page inside Twenty's Settings area. The merchant operator who uses it manages value-added tax policies: one policy carries two rates — مالیات means tax and عوارض means duty — plus a computed total, and a single active default policy. Documents, پیش‌فاکتور meaning quotation, are priced with a policy. A policy that has been used cannot be deleted, only deactivated.

Two design directions were previously generated by this same design service and they are attached as context: draft-a-compact-table.html and draft-b-card-grid.html. The shipped implementation blended them. The operator rejected the shipped result.

The operator's own words, verbatim:

«طراحی قبلی را رد می‌کنم. دکمه‌ها و متن‌های صفحه از هم قابل تشخیص نیستند، صفحه شلوغ است و نمی‌فهمم کدام چیز را باید بزنم.»

Meaning: I reject the previous design. The buttons and the text on the page cannot be told apart, the page is cluttered, and I cannot tell what I am supposed to click.

## Your job in this step

Diagnose the screen independently. Do not propose CSS values yet and do not accept any solution handed to you. Work from the actual screen, the attached component source, and Twenty's own design language.

Cover at least these areas.

1. Interaction hierarchy. Why can an operator not tell an action from a label. Which controls compete with each other. What is the correct visual precedence for the actions this screen really needs: edit a policy, make a policy the default, deactivate or reactivate, delete an unused policy, add a policy, save and cancel.
2. Visual hierarchy and scannability. With four numbers per policy — tax, duty, total, document count — plus a name and a status, what should lead and what should recede. Where does the current layout make the eye work for no reason.
3. Density and redundant microcopy. Which repeated sentences, hints and captions earn their space. Which must stay because they carry a legal or financial consequence, for example that a used policy is deactivated rather than deleted.
4. RTL and Persian typography. Spacing, alignment, number and percent rendering with the Persian percent sign, label placement, and where an RTL reading order conflicts with the current order.
5. States. Active, inactive, default, used and unused must each be recognisable at a glance, and the default policy must be unmistakable.
6. Mobile at 390 px. What must collapse, what must stay, and how the actions should present on a phone without horizontal scrolling.

Close with one concrete recommended direction and a short list of non-negotiable acceptance criteria an implementation must satisfy for you to call it faithful.

## Format of your output

Produce ONE desktop RTL page, dir rtl, Persian, light theme, that is a readable design review document:

- a title and a one-line verdict,
- findings grouped by severity, each with the symptom an operator actually sees,
- your recommended direction, with a small annotated schematic of the recommended row and its action area — an inline sketch is fine,
- the acceptance criteria as a checklist.

Use Twenty's own visual language for the page itself, see the attached theme tokens. No lorem ipsum. Keep it dense and plain, like an internal design note, not a marketing page.
